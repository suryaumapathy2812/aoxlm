# CEFR Speaking Assessment - Labeling Guidelines

## Version 1.0 | February 2026

---

## 1. Introduction

### Purpose

These guidelines help human raters consistently assess English speaking proficiency using a simplified 4-level CEFR scale (A1, A2, B1, B2+).

### Important Notes

1. We use a **4-class system**: A1, A2, B1, B2+ (not the full 6-class A1-C2)
2. B2+ includes B2, C1, and C2 (we don't distinguish between them)
3. We assess **Indian English learners** from various L1 backgrounds
4. **Don't penalize valid Indian English patterns** (see L1 Patterns Reference)

---

## 2. The 4-Class CEFR Scale

### A1 - Beginner

**Can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and very basic phrases.**

| Dimension | Characteristics |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Fluency** | Very slow speech (<60 WPM), long pauses (>3s), isolated words, frequent breakdowns |
| **Range** | Basic vocabulary only (<500 words), greetings, numbers, common objects |
| **Accuracy** | Only memorized phrases correct, systematic errors in all structures |
| **Phonology** | Individual sounds often unclear, heavy L1 accent, communication often impaired |
| **Coherence** | Isolated words/phrases, no connectors, cannot maintain topic |

**Example A1 Speech:**
> "I... name... [3s pause]... Ravi. I... Chennai. [2s pause] Work... office. [4s pause] Family... wife... two children."

---

### A2 - Elementary

**Can communicate in simple and routine tasks requiring simple exchange of information.**

| Dimension | Characteristics |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Fluency** | Slow but mostly continuous (60-100 WPM), noticeable hesitations, short sentences, self-corrections common |
| **Range** | Everyday vocabulary (500-1000 words), familiar topics (family, work, local area) |
| **Accuracy** | Simple structures sometimes correct, frequent errors but meaning usually clear |
| **Phonology** | Generally intelligible despite accent, some sounds unclear, basic intonation |
| **Coherence** | Simple connectors (and, but, so, because), can sequence events, some topic drift |

**Example A2 Speech:**
> "I grew up in Chennai only. It was small small place. But later I shifted to new place. Now it is big place and surroundings are very friendly. So I like that place because everything is there. Shops are nearby nearby."

---

### B1 - Intermediate

**Can deal with most situations likely to arise while traveling or discussing familiar topics.**

| Dimension | Characteristics |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Fluency** | Reasonable speech rate (100-130 WPM), maintains flow despite some hesitation, can produce longer stretches |
| **Range** | Vocabulary for work, school, leisure (1000-2000 words), can describe experiences and events |
| **Accuracy** | Good control of simple structures, errors in complex ones, communication not impeded |
| **Phonology** | Clearly intelligible, occasional unclear sounds, appropriate intonation patterns |
| **Coherence** | Range of connectors, can maintain topic, logical sequencing, some complex sentences |

**Example B1 Speech:**
> "I grew up in Chennai, which is a large city in South India. When I was a child, we lived in a small neighborhood where everyone knew each other. Later, we moved to a more developed area because my father got a new job. I have many fond memories of playing with my friends and going to the local temple during festivals."

---

### B2+ - Upper Intermediate and Above

**Can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity, produce clear detailed text on a wide range of subjects.**

| Dimension | Characteristics |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Fluency** | Natural speech rate (>130 WPM), fluent and spontaneous, rare hesitation only for complex ideas |
| **Range** | Wide vocabulary including abstract terms (>2000 words), can express nuance, uses idioms |
| **Accuracy** | High degree of grammatical control, rare errors usually self-corrected |
| **Phonology** | Near-native intelligibility, appropriate stress and intonation, clear articulation |
| **Coherence** | Sophisticated discourse structure, varied connectors, can build arguments, nuanced connections |

**Example B2+ Speech:**
> "Growing up in Chennai was a transformative experience that fundamentally shaped my worldview. The city, with its unique blend of traditional values and modern aspirations, provided a rich tapestry of experiences. What I find particularly remarkable in retrospect is how the rapid urbanization I witnessed during my childhood mirrors the broader trajectory of India's development."

## 3. Rating Each Dimension

### 3.1 Fluency

**What to assess**: Speech rate, pauses, hesitations, repetitions, self-corrections

| Level | WPM | Pause Ratio | Characteristics |
|-------|-----|-------------|-----------------|
| A1 | <60 | >30% | Isolated words, constant pauses |
| A2 | 60-100 | 15-30% | Simple sentences, frequent hesitations |
| B1 | 100-130 | 5-15% | Extended speech, occasional pauses |
| B2+ | >130 | <5% | Fluent, natural pace |

**Counting Guide**:
- Count pauses >0.5 seconds
- Count repetitions (word repeated immediately)
- Count self-corrections ("I went... I go to...")
- Note: Reduplication ("small small") is L1 pattern, not fluency issue

### 3.2 Range (Vocabulary)

**What to assess**: Vocabulary diversity, word sophistication, ability to express ideas

| Level | TTR* | Characteristics |
|-------|------|-----------------|
| A1 | <0.30 | Basic words only, very repetitive |
| A2 | 0.30-0.45 | Everyday vocabulary, some repetition |
| B1 | 0.45-0.55 | Topic vocabulary, moderate diversity |
| B2+ | >0.55 | Wide range, precise word choice |

*TTR = Type-Token Ratio (unique words / total words)

**Look for**:
- Vocabulary variety (or lack of)
- Ability to paraphrase when needed
- Use of topic-specific words
- Vague words ("thing", "stuff") vs. precise terms

### 3.3 Accuracy (Grammar)

**What to assess**: Grammatical correctness, word forms, sentence structure

| Level | Error Rate | Characteristics |
|-------|------------|-----------------|
| A1 | >15/100 words | Systematic errors, communication impaired |
| A2 | 10-15/100 words | Frequent errors, meaning usually clear |
| B1 | 5-10/100 words | Some errors, don't impede communication |
| B2+ | <5/100 words | Rare errors, usually self-corrected |

**Common error types**:
- Subject-verb agreement: "He go" → "He goes"
- Tense errors: "Yesterday I go" → "Yesterday I went"
- Article errors: "He is engineer" → "He is an engineer"
- Word form: "clearful" → "clear"

**DON'T count as errors**:
- L1 transfer patterns (see L1 Patterns Reference)
- "Chennai only", "small small", "I am having" (valid Indian English)

### 3.4 Phonology

**What to assess**: Pronunciation, intelligibility, intonation, stress

| Level | Intelligibility | Characteristics |
|-------|-----------------|-----------------|
| A1 | Often unclear | Communication frequently impaired |
| A2 | Generally clear | Noticeable accent, mostly understood |
| B1 | Clearly intelligible | Occasional unclear words |
| B2+ | Highly intelligible | Native-like clarity |

**Important**: 
- Indian English has its own phonological system
- Retroflex consonants are a FEATURE, not an error
- Focus on INTELLIGIBILITY, not "native-like" accent
- Don't penalize Indian English pronunciation patterns

### 3.5 Coherence

**What to assess**: Logical flow, discourse markers, topic maintenance

| Level | Characteristics |
|-------|-----------------|
| A1 | Isolated phrases, no connectors, cannot maintain topic |
| A2 | Basic connectors (and, but, so), can sequence, some drift |
| B1 | Range of connectors, maintains topic, logical flow |
| B2+ | Sophisticated structure, builds arguments, nuanced connections |

**Discourse markers to look for**:
- Additive: and, also, moreover, furthermore
- Contrastive: but, however, although, on the other hand
- Causal: because, so, therefore, as a result
- Temporal: then, after, before, finally
- Exemplifying: for example, such as, like

**Note**: Heavy use of "So" is common in Indian English - don't over-penalize.

## 4. Rating Procedure

### Step 1: Listen First

1. Listen to the entire audio without rating
2. Get an overall impression
3. Note any standout features (good or bad)

### Step 2: Rate Overall Level

1. What's your gut feeling for overall level?
2. This helps calibrate dimension ratings

### Step 3: Rate Each Dimension

1. Listen again, focusing on one dimension at a time
2. Use the criteria tables above
3. Consider: What level does this BEST fit?

### Step 4: Verify Consistency

1. Do dimension ratings make sense together?
2. Is overall rating consistent with dimension ratings?
3. If not, re-evaluate

### Step 5: Document

1. Fill in all required columns
2. Add notes for anything unusual
3. Flag if you're uncertain

## 5. Edge Cases & Difficult Decisions

### When between two levels

If a speaker seems between A2 and B1:
1. List evidence for each level
2. Which level has MORE evidence?
3. When truly split, lean toward lower level (conservative)

### When dimensions differ greatly

Example: Fluency B1, Accuracy A1

This can happen! Rate each dimension independently based on evidence.

### When audio quality is poor

1. If you can still assess → rate with "acceptable" quality flag
2. If some dimensions unclear → rate what you can, note limitations
3. If unusable → mark as "unusable", don't rate

### When L1 patterns are heavy

1. Identify if patterns are systematic L1 transfer
2. Don't penalize L1 patterns in Accuracy
3. May still affect Fluency perception (hesitation patterns)
4. Note in comments which patterns you observed

## 6. Calibration

### Weekly Calibration Sessions

1. All raters review 5 samples together
2. Compare ratings before discussing
3. Discuss disagreements
4. Update understanding of criteria

### Inter-Rater Reliability (IRR)

- 20% of samples are double-rated
- Target: >70% exact agreement
- Target: >90% within-one-level agreement
- Disagreements are adjudicated by senior rater

### When to Flag for Discussion

- You're very uncertain about a rating
- The case doesn't fit the criteria well
- You see a pattern not covered in guidelines
- Audio quality is borderline

## 7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|------------|
| Penalizing Indian English | Check L1 Patterns Reference first |
| Expecting native pronunciation | Focus on intelligibility, not accent |
| Over-penalizing "So" usage | Common in Indian English discourse |
| Rating all dimensions the same | Each dimension is independent |
| Letting one bad moment define rating | Consider overall performance |
| Being too harsh on A1/A2 | Remember, these are beginners |
| Being too lenient on B1/B2+ | Maintain standards for higher levels |

---

## 8. Quality Flags

### Audio Quality

| Flag | Meaning | Action |
|------|---------|--------|
| good | Clear audio, minimal noise | Rate normally |
| acceptable | Some noise but speech clear | Rate with caution |
| poor | Significant noise, some words unclear | Rate what you can, note limitations |
| unusable | Cannot reliably assess | Don't rate, flag for re-recording |

### Rating Confidence

| Flag | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| high | Clear case, confident in rating |
| medium | Some uncertainty, but reasonable assessment |
| low | Difficult case, significant uncertainty |

1. Audio won't play → Note and skip
2. Form issues → Contact project lead
3. Unclear task → Check task_type and task_prompt columns
